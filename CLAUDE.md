# wav2vec2bert-jyutping Development Guide

## Project Goal and Branches

Fine-tune `facebook/w2v-bert-2.0` for Cantonese Jyutping recognition (audio -> toned Jyutping syllables).

| Branch | What it is |
|---|---|
| `main` | Published model `hon9kon9ize/wav2vec2bert-jyutping`: two CTC heads (`jyutping_head` = onset/final pieces without tone, `tone_head` = tone digits). Its model card says Common Voice 17, but it scores 0.01% SER on `dataset_emo_jyutping`'s test split, so it trained on that dataset (see Results). |
| `neclueus-tone` (typo in the name) | **Latest Cantonese-only work**: one CTC head over nucleus+tone tokens, ONNX serving, error analysis, head-design comparison harness. |
| `multilingual` | Experiment: one head over yue/zh/en phoneme tokens. Not working yet (see Known Issues). |

## Dataset and Annotation

- Default dataset: `indiejoseph/tts20250516` (a TTS-training corpus; 261,524 clips, 16 kHz). Columns used: `audio`, `phone` (inline toned Jyutping, e.g. `zung3 so2 zau1 zi1 .`). `text` (the Chinese sentence) is also present and is only used for label checks; the rest (`speech_token`, `spk_emb`, `phone_token_ids`, ...) is ignored.
- Split: `dataset_utils.make_train_validation_test_split` shuffles with seed 42, then 500 test, 500 validation, the rest train. Never change it: results are only comparable on this split.
- Clips are short: mean 2.2 s, 95th percentile 4.0 s, max 14.5 s; only 0.28% are longer than 7 s.
- **Labels are not human annotation.** Per the dataset owner, both corpora were labelled by previously trained models with ToJyutping (a text-to-jyutping converter) as the reference. So `phone` / `labels` are pseudo-labels: SER is agreement with that labelling, not accuracy against what was said; where a speaker deviates from the canonical reading the label can be wrong while the model is right; mistakes shared with the labelling models are invisible; and a published model scoring 0.00% on emo may simply match its own lineage's labels (unverified). Both datasets carry the Chinese `text` ToJyutping reads. Measured with `check_labels_vs_tojyutping.py` (ToJyutping 3.2.0, all 301,886 clips): the label has exactly the syllable count of ToJyutping(text) in 100% of clips (so labels follow the text, not free ASR output), but only 75.6% (tts) / 52.7% (emo) of sentences are identical and 97.0% / 94.9% of syllables agree; the deviations are colloquial or polyphone variants (`nei1` vs `ni1`, `lei4` vs `lai4`, `gok3` vs `gwok3`, `wui5` vs `wui6`, `wai4` vs `wai6`...), 64% / 80% of them tone, and the two datasets deviate in different words (different labelling pipelines). Only the 6 hand-checked wavs measure real accuracy. Known consequences: colloquial mergers (`n/l`, `ng/∅`, `gw/g`, tones `3/6`, `4/6`, `2/5`) are scored as errors; a few conversion mistakes exist (e.g. 擦 `caat4`, 賊 `caak2`); 385 tokens of stop-final syllables carry a tone outside 1/3/6 (mostly tone 2, e.g. `dip2`, `guk2`; most likely genuine changed tones, not errors).
- Since 2026-10-17 part of the label noise has been audited by ear (Results So Far, "Label audit by listening"): 28 per-character corrections touch 0.32% of the tts syllables. They are kept as override files, never written into the dataset, and the fixed test split keeps its original labels.
- Syllables without an onset are 0.49% of data (`aa3`, `on1`), syllabic nasals (`m4`, `ng5`) 0.94%. The dataset's own phone arrays have no placeholder for a missing slot, and we do not add one (see Decisions).
- All 261,524 rows parse and every label token is in `vocab_nucleus_tone.json`.
- The audio's origin (synthesized vs recorded) is not established: clip lengths are continuous, not locked to the 25 Hz speech-token grid, which is more typical of recordings, but only the dataset's owner knows.
- Second corpus, only on genkai: `/home/pj24001684/ku40000295/jc/projects/usm/dataset_emo_jyutping` (85 GB; `train` 40,362 + `test` 1,035 clips; 22.05 kHz; median 3.1 s). Columns: `audio`, `text` (simplified-script written Cantonese, ~13% of rows carry an emoji), `labels` (inline toned jyutping, e.g. `daan6 hai6 bing6 m4 ...`), `jyutping` (tone-less pieces), `tone`. It is what the original two-head scripts (`jyutping` + `tone` columns) read, and the published model's training data. Use it with `--mix_dataset <path>:labels` (train split only; its test split stays held out; the published model has seen it).
- Common Voice 17 Cantonese (public mirror `fsicoli/common_voice_17_0`, in the local HF cache) has **no jyutping**, only Chinese `sentence` text. Converting it with pycantonese or similar gives too many label errors for training, so it is not used. It could only serve as a rough relative yardstick for evaluation.

## Label Format (single head) - important

One token stream: onset tokens (tone-less) and rime+tone tokens, with `|` **only between syllables**:

```text
zung3 so2 zau1 zi1   ->   z ung3 | s o2 | z au1 | z i1
```

- Vocab (281): `[PAD]`=0 (CTC blank, never in a label), `[UNK]`=1, `|`=2, 19 onsets (`b c d f g gw h j k kw l m n ng p s t w z`), 257 rime+tone tokens (`aa4`, `ung3`, ...), plus 2 tokenizer extras. Syllables with no onset (`aa3`) and syllabic nasals (`m4`, `ng5`) are one token.
- Build labels with `jyutping.annotation_to_nucleus_tone_label_tokens(...)` and map with `tokenizer.convert_tokens_to_ids(tokens)`.
- **Do not** build labels with `processor(text="m aa4 m aa1")`: Wav2Vec2CTCTokenizer turns every space into `|`, so a `|` lands between each onset and its rime (the old checkpoints were trained that way; targets are ~35% longer).
- Decoding: `ctc_utils.ctc_ids_to_token_text(tokenizer, ids, length)` collapses repeats, drops blanks and `|`, and gives `z ung3 s o2 ...`; `jyutping.nucleus_tone_text_to_jyutping` pairs onsets with rimes. Both label styles decode to the same text, so old and new checkpoints are scored identically.
- Always pass the real CTC output `length` when decoding a padded batch; the model emits ids for padding frames. `LengthAwareTrainer` returns `(logits, output_lengths)` for this reason.
- `load_ctc_tokenizer` keeps multi-character tokens whole (the `AddedToken` patch). Reuse it; the training/eval scripts still carry their own copy.
- CTC blank is `processor.tokenizer.pad_token_id`; keep `[PAD]` at ID 0. `len(tokenizer)` is vocab entries + 2, which is the head size.

## Training Recipe

**One learning rate for everything does not work at small batch sizes**: with these labels the loss sits at ~3 (all-blank plateau, SER ~100%). Give the output head its own, higher rate:

```bash
python train_w2v2bert_nucleus_tone.py facebook/w2v-bert-2.0 --output_dir checkpoints_nucleus_tone \
  --learning_rate 5e-5 --head_learning_rate 1e-3 --per_device_train_batch_size 16 --warmup_steps 100 --precision bf16
```

- Validated only at batch 16: 300 steps -> 18.7% test SER; 1,000 steps on 6,000 clips -> 4.8%; **full data, 30,000 steps (run20, base init, H100, ~7 steps/s, 1h16) -> 1.75% test SER**, matching the old 6-epoch run at about a third of its samples. Validation SER curve of run20: 6.2% (step 2k) -> 3.1% (10k) -> 1.7% (22k) -> 1.2% (30k); it was still improving when the linear decay hit zero. The original defaults (batch 256, lr 1e-3, uniform) are untested with these labels: watch the first 300 steps (loss should fall below ~1.5; a flat ~3.0 means the plateau).
- Fixed in the model setup: adapter on (`add_adapter=True`), `ctc_zero_infinity=True`, dropout 0.2, `mask_time_prob=0.0` (**no SpecAugment**; untested change worth trying).
- The script's other defaults: `num_train_epochs=10` hard-coded, precision fp16 by default (bf16 on Ampere/H100), `--eval_steps` default 1000. The best full-data run (2026-05-09, genkai) used 6 epochs and eval every 508 steps.
- Checkpoint choice: the validation SER logged by older runs was inflated by decoding padding frames, so "best checkpoint" picks from those runs are unreliable. `checkpoint-3048` (final step) is as good as the old "best" (test SER 1.75% vs 1.84%).

## Evaluation

- Always report syllable error rate (SER) on full toned Jyutping, on the fixed 500-clip test split, with the length-aware decode:
  `python eval_w2v2bert_nucleus_tone.py --model_id <ckpt> --split test --batch_size 1`
  (published model: `python eval_original_w2v2bert.py --model_id <dir> --split test --batch_size 1`).
- Do not compare old `per`/`ter` with single-stream `per`; the units differ. Compare SER.
- The test split is in-domain corpus audio; it says little about audio from elsewhere. `tmp/labels.md` lists 6 hand-labelled wavs (79 syllables) for a quick check; it is far too small for decisions, and its labels must be checked against the audio: three of the 79 were wrong (媽媽 is spoken `maa4 maa1`, 呢 `ne1`, a missing `maai4`) and were found by comparing models and measuring pitch. Label noise of ~4% is larger than the in-domain error rate.
- **Synthetic rare-word eval** (`/home/joseph/projects/qwen3asr-analysis/synthetic/rare_eval`, `score_rare_eval.py`; needs ToJyutping, e.g. `--tojyutping_path` of a copy of the anaconda 3.2.0 package): 294 JyutVoice clips (16 kHz, 24 voices, mean 4.0 s), 4,733 syllables, 893 of them in 362 rare target words. Labels are `ToJyutping(text)` plus the manifest's `g2p_fix` (the JyutVoice frontend's colloquial corrections, `qwen3asr-analysis/jyutvoice_patches/colloquial.py`: 40 clips, 40 syllables, e.g. 無諗 `mou4`->`mou5`; the script scores both labels, the fixed one is primary; 7 listed fixes match nothing because ToJyutping 3.2.0 already reads the corrected value or the entry is a duplicate). Labels are exact for the phone string given, not for the audio (the TTS inherits reading noise), so this is a stress test of rare words and unseen voices, not an accuracy measure. Result (2026-10-15): run21 8.47% SER (plain ToJyutping 8.94%; 83/294 clips exact; target-word syllables 16.6%, other syllables 6.6%; per-voice 4.4-11.9%; 264 of 362 target words error-free), run20 10.04% (target 19.0%, 68 clips exact). Of run21's 396 substitutions 46% are tone-only, 21% rime-only, 19% onset-only, 30% differ only by a merger pair; the top confusions are the labeller coin-flips (呢 `ni1`->`nei1`/`ne1` x13, 下 `haa5`->`haa6` x7, 嚟 `lai4`->`lei4` x5) and n->l (`nan2`->`lan2` x11, all on 撚). Not compared with the published model (weights not in the local HF cache).
- Audio-perturbation probes: use plain resampling for pitch/speed changes. Librosa's phase-vocoder `pitch_shift`/`time_stretch` damages recognition for every model even when pitch and speed are restored, so it says nothing about pitch robustness.

Test = the fixed 500-clip `tts20250516` split. emo test = `dataset_emo_jyutping`'s 1,035-clip test split (13,344 syllables). Wavs = the 6 hand-labelled clips (79 syllables, labels corrected against the audio).

| **run20**: base init, 30k steps x bs 16 (`checkpoints/run20_best`, 1h16 on an H100) | 1.75% (PER 0.90%) | 3.05% | 17 (21.5%) |

- **The published model trained on `dataset_emo_jyutping`**, not (only) Common Voice: 1 error in 13,344 syllables on that test split. Its own logged eval PER 4.2% / TER 8.7% came from the padding bug; the true error is ~0. So "published = real speech, ours = TTS" is wrong; both trained on TTS-like corpora. The published model is not a fair yardstick on `emo test`.
- Wavs: ours and the published model tie on the 5 short clips (5/45 errors each); the published lead is the 7 s casual clip `叮噹.wav` (9 vs 12 errors). 79 syllables cannot separate them.
- **Investigation of the wav errors (the "real-speech gap" is not established).** Ruled out: label-convention memorisation (only 1 of 19 errors is the training annotation's majority reading), near-digital silence / loudness / noise floor (no effect), noise (30-10 dB SNR) and 4 kHz bandwidth (robust), pitch/speed changes done by plain resampling (robust to +-2 semitones). 12 of 27 syllables wrong in at least one model are wrong in all three (different data and recipes), often with the same wrong answer; one is a proven label/audio mismatch. Remaining errors concentrate in `叮噹.wav` (rare words such as 噹, and `l/n`, `p/b` confusions).
- **Transfer learning works in-domain**: starting from the published encoder cut light-run test errors by 36% (4.82% -> 3.09%, paired 95% CI -2.44 to -1.02 points) and trained ~4x faster; the effect on the wavs (-4 syllables) is within noise. Encoder interpolation (published encoder <-> that fine-tune, head from the fine-tune) is smooth: alpha 0 -> 1 takes in-domain SER 6.1% -> 2.9% (200 clips) while wav errors move 17 -> 19; no demonstrated benefit on the wavs yet.
- **run21** (published encoder + `--mix_dataset dataset_emo_jyutping:labels`, 2026-10-10, genkai c-batch H100, ~6 steps/s): validation SER 4.13% (step 2k) -> 2.06% (20k) -> 1.41% (36k, best) -> 1.47% (40k, final); `checkpoint-36000` (chosen by validation) scores test 1.60% / emo test 1.83% / wavs 13 errors, the final `checkpoint-40000` 1.53% / 1.71% / 12. The two checkpoints differ by a few syllables (noise), so use the final one. Against run20: test 1.75% -> 1.53%, wavs 17 -> 12 errors, and the first model here to beat the published one on the wavs (14); all three differences are small (no paired test run; 79 wav syllables), only the direction is consistent. Its validation SER was above run20's at the same step in the middle of training (1.7% vs 1.2% at 30k) yet the test split ended better, so compare final numbers, not mid-run curves. The emo test is not clean for run21 either: its encoder comes from the model that trained on that dataset.
- **Where run21 is weak** (checkpoint-40000 on 1,000 held-out tts clips = 9,250 syllables, SER 1.55%, and on emo test, 13,344 syllables, SER 1.71%; 143 / 228 errors, so per-token rates below rest on a handful of errors): tone is 65% / 76% of substitutions (tone wrong in 1.0% / 1.3% of syllables, onset 0.35% / 0.21%, rime 0.27%); 95% of substitutions are wrong in one part only. About 60% of tone errors are the merger pairs 3<->6, 4<->6, 2<->5 (3->6 alone 25 / 23), 66-72% are within one pitch level, and T1 is almost never wrong (0.2-0.3%). Onset errors are mostly n<->l (l->n 2.2% of gold `l`, n->l 3.1% of gold `n` on emo); rime errors are mostly e<->a (e 0.8%), codas are almost never wrong. **Half the errors (50% / 48%) differ from the gold only by a merger pair (tone 3/6, 4/6, 2/5; onset n/l, ng/-, gw/g)**: without them SER would be 0.78% / 0.89%; the labels are canonical readings, so some of these may be the speaker's real pronunciation. Error rate falls steadily with training frequency of the gold syllable (>=10k occurrences 0.6-0.7%, 1k-10k 1.3-1.7%, 100-1k 4-5%, 10-100 15-24%, <10 50%+): syllables seen <1,000 times are 8-11% of the tokens but 29-45% of the errors. Worst single syllables: `lei4`<->`lai4` (25-36%), `wai6`->`wai4`, `zoi3`->`zoi6`, `dou2`->`dou3`, `si4`->`si6`. Sentence-final syllables are weaker (2.3% tts, 3.4% emo vs 1.3-1.5% in the middle). The 6-wav errors mostly are not these types: m->b, d->z, p->b, g->f never occur in-domain (0 of 370 / 790 / 70 / 1,260 gold occurrences) and T1<->T3/T6 is rare (0.1-0.3%); only `soeng6->soeng4` (T6->T4) and the `l->n` in `lai4->nin4` are in-domain-typical, so the wav gap is not explained by the label-convention pairs.
- **Cross-dataset consistency** (`error_consistency.py`, 2026-10-10; tts held-out 9,250 syllables, emo test 13,344, plus 1,000 training clips of each): run21 is not fitted to one dataset: held-out onset/rime/tone rates agree across tts and emo (0.35/0.27/0.99% vs 0.21/0.27/1.27%, no significant difference), tone confusions are the same pairs in both (cosine 0.86), and its training-clip error (1.06% tts, 1.49% emo) is close to held-out (1.55%, 1.71%), with 42-48% of it merger pairs, so the ~1% floor is label ambiguity, not memorisation. Dataset dependence is in tone then rime, not onset: run20 (tts only) on emo goes 1.51% -> 3.05% with onset unchanged, rime x2.1, tone x2.5 (gold tones 6/4/5/2 fail 2-4x as often; 3->6 does not change); the published model (emo only) on tts degrades everywhere (SER 5.49%; onset x3.6, rime x5.5, tone x3.2; 18 of 25 gold-`g` errors are `g`->`gw`, a labelling difference between the corpora). The published model scores 0.00-0.02% on both an emo training sample and the emo test, so that test is effectively seen. The 6 wavs fail differently from both datasets (onset is 7 of 12 errors vs 13-23% of substitutions in the datasets): the real overfitting risk is a third domain.
- **Characters with several readings explain most of the error** (`particle_analysis.py`; a reviewer noted 呢 `ne1`/`ni1`/`nei1` depends on context and emotion): the 835 of 5,231 characters with >= 2 readings attested (>= 3% of their training labels) are ~20% of syllables but 63-64% of run21's errors (rate 4.9-5.1% vs 0.7-0.8% for the rest); 57% (tts) / 72% (emo) of the errors are another attested reading of the same character. Reading-tolerant SER: tts 1.52% -> 0.65%, emo 1.69% -> 0.46% (lower bounds; the 3% threshold is generous). The labellers are near coin-flips on 呢 (ne1 59 / nei1 28 / ni1 11 / le1 3%), 嚟 (lei4 54 / lai4 46%), 在 (zoi6 63 / zoi3 35%), 為 (wai4 53 / wai6 47%). Report a reading-tolerant SER next to the strict one.
- **The tone pairs 3/6, 4/6, 2/5 are documented ongoing mergers in Hong Kong Cantonese** (Mok & Wong 2010, CUHK, https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/lin/people/peggy/PCC2010.pdf: of 17 potential mergers, 9 merged T2/T5, 9 T3/T6, 3 T4/T6; the tone space is crowded in the lower range (T3/T6 about 30 Hz apart, T4/T6 differ only in a slight fall); merging is gradual, in either direction, and varies by speaker), so part of these "errors" is real variation that canonical labels cannot record. `tone_merger_patterns.py` on run21's held-out errors: direction is lopsided on tts (T3->T6 24 vs T6->T3 6) but balanced on emo; errors barely cluster within clips; the rate falls with syllable training frequency (0.3% for >= 10k occurrences vs 3.6-14% for < 100), which is not comparable with the paper's word-frequency effect. Speaker structure cannot be recovered (per-clip tts speaker embeddings form no clear clusters; no speaker ids). Report a merger-tolerant SER next to the strict one; a lexical (word-level) model is the principled lever, and both datasets carry `text`.
- **The model is nearly categorical on the merger pairs** (`tone_ambiguity.py`; Mok & Wong 2010 Table 3 codes merging speakers' T2/T5 tokens as target / partner / between: T2->T5 14%, T5->T2 4%, between 2-4%): on syllables whose gold tone is in a merger pair, 98-99% have posterior share >= 0.9 on the gold tone, ~1% sit between, < 1% go to the partner (10-30x below the paper's merging speakers). One-hot canonical labels plus a saturated CTC posterior cannot express graded realisations; graded (soft) targets for these pairs are untried.
- **Synthetic data (JyutVoice TTS, `~/projects/notebooks/notebooks/bert-vits2/frankenstein-matcha2`, env `yue-zipformer` + packages added with `uv pip --target`; TTS 5 diffusion steps = 0.23 s/clip, bf16 slower, run <= 3 processes x 2 threads) and augmentation, tested in light runs (`run_se*.sh`, `compare_heads.py --extra_train_dir ... --extra_train_ref_prefix ref`)**: natural-sentence synthetic clips cut real rare-syllable errors (< 1,000 uses) by 10-16% in both seeds; the 1,500 rare-heavy subset does it with no cost beyond noise (tts +8/+14, emo -9/+20 vs same-seed baselines), the generic 3,777 pool costs +25 to +46 tts errors; masked-LM generated sentences and augmentation (noise/echo/reverb/speed) add nothing for real data. Run-to-run noise is about +-20 errors on tts/emo. The TTS inherits the labels' reading noise (呢 asked as `ne1` is heard as `ni1`), so synthetic labels are exact only for the phone string given, not the audio: build synthetic sentences with `select_synth_sentences.py --reliable 0.85` (every character used with a reading covering >= 85% of its uses). Never use the labelled wavs as TTS voices (speaker leak into the only real-speech test); the TTS front end needs one syllable per character (normalize to chars + "。" and syllables + "."). Verified by ear (30 real clips where the synthetic-trained model newly disagreed with a canonical label): label right in 24 (80%), model right in 6 (20%, ~9-37%) - the rise is mostly real degradation, and ~1 in 5 such disagreements is a label error. Full-scale gain estimated at ~4-6% of all errors, so a dedicated genkai run is not justified without a larger verified real-speech eval set; adding the rare-heavy subset to the next scheduled full run is cheap.
- **Graded (merger-aware) targets hurt** (`compare_heads.py --merger_smoothing`, `run_ms.sh`, `merger_smoothing_report.py`; five light runs, single seed): weights 0.005-0.05 all give +35 tts errors (2.92% -> 3.30%, CI excludes 0) with the same error although the partner share runs 0.7-14%, so part is seed noise; 0.15 gives +93; tone-error-detection AUROC drops from 0.96 to 0.88-0.92; baseline + character-LM decoder (120 test errors) still beats every smoothed model + decoder (121-144). Do not spread probability over merger pairs with a loss term; graded targets would need graded labels.
- **Lexical context does not resolve the merger pairs** (`lexical_experiment.py`, 2026-10-10): (A) a reader that knows the TRUE characters and gives each the syllable the labellers most often gave it (with neighbours as context) reproduces the labels worse (SER 2.0% tts val, 2.4% tts test, 3.1% emo; character only 3.7-5.0%) than the model (1.5-1.7%), so the labels are not determined by the text (they came from models that heard the audio); (B) a latent-character decoder (model tone posterior at the rime peak x character 4-gram LM, beta tuned on tts_val) removes ~11% of the merger-pair tone errors (146 -> 130) but breaks other syllables: net 366 -> 360 errors (tts test -6, emo +6). Weak test (no end-to-end training, one frame per syllable), so a jointly trained text-supervised model is not ruled out, but large gains from text look unlikely.
- In-domain errors: tone ~2/3 of substituted syllables, then onset (mostly same-place pairs like `l/n`, `z/s`), then rime; 96% of substitutions are wrong in one part only.
- Light head comparison (`compare_heads.py`, 6,000 clips, 1,000 steps, identical recipe): single 4.84%, two-head 4.56%, single + auxiliary tone loss 4.75% test SER. No significant SER differences; two-head gets more clips exactly right (69.8% vs 64.8%, p~0.015); two-head tone/syllable length mismatch 0.6%.

## Decisions (do not revisit without new evidence)

- Keep the single head. No measurable SER cost vs two heads, and no tone-stream sync failure.
- No auxiliary tone head (no net gain: fewer tone errors, more rime errors), no three-head design.
- No placeholder token for syllables without an initial or final: rare (1.4%), already unambiguous via `|`, models get syllabic nasals 40/40 right.
- **Best model and serving model: `checkpoints/run22_8000` (run22; user decision 2026-10-19).** run21 continued for 8,000 steps on the corrected labels (28 rules; only the top 8 of 24 encoder layers + adapter + head trained; 94,006 clips = 72,003 tts [7,003 corrected, repeated twice] + 15,000 emo; the 20,120 judged clips left out; genkai b-batch-mig job 7107230). Why: on the 501 audio-judged items run22 is right in 80% vs 69% for run21 (it fixes 70 of its 153 errors and keeps 330 of 348 right answers); on real speech it is level with run21 (see Results, hard errors 4.01% vs 4.03%); emo test 1.74% vs 1.71%. What it does not win: the fixed tts test split (1.82% vs 1.53%: its labels still carry the corrected errors, 20 of the 84 errors sit at corrected positions) and the 6 wavs (13 vs 12 errors of 79, noise). Known side effects not yet checked by ear: the 3->6 spillover onto 再/載/置, 下 haa6 -> haa5, gw -> g on 過/國 (see Results). `serve.py`, `export_onnx.py` and `docker-compose.onnx.yml` default to run22 (`onnx/run22/`: fp32 + int8; parity max |logit diff| 3.3e-5, argmax identical; onnxruntime decoding equals PyTorch on the 6 wavs). Re-export with `python export_onnx.py` after changing the checkpoint. **run21 stays on disk untouched** (`checkpoints/run21_40000`, `onnx/model.onnx[.data]`) because the separate playground container (`../wav2vec2-jyutping-playground`, port 8001) mounts them: it still serves run21; to switch it, point its mounts to `onnx/run22` and `checkpoints/run22_8000` and restart it (nothing in that repo was edited). The weights are not in git (`checkpoints/` is ignored): copy them from genkai `relabel_exp/out/retrain_top8/final` (the model files only) to rebuild run22, and from `checkpoints_mix_pubinit/checkpoint-40000` for run21.
- No jyutping labels generated by a character-to-jyutping converter (pycantonese or similar) for training: too many conversion errors. Only corpora that already carry jyutping labels (`tts20250516`, `dataset_emo_jyutping`) are used.
- Initialise from the published encoder (transfer learning): validated in a light run and at scale by `run21` (mixed corpora; test 1.53%, best model on the wavs so far). It was the serving model until run22 (see above).
- Correct labels per character with `relabel_rules.py`, never by blanket "model and ToJyutping agree" (ToJyutping is wrong on colloquial changed tones such as 到 dou2 and 位 wai2). The corrections stay override files (`--phone_override`); the dataset and the fixed test split are never edited. Where a rule needs the model it applies only if the model says the corrected reading.

## Gotchas

- `.gitignore` ignores `*.json` and `*.sh`; vocab files need `git add -f`. `checkpoints/`, `wandb/`, `onnx/`, `tmp/` are ignored but `checkpoints_*` directories are not.
- Local `checkpoints/` was cleaned on 2026-10-15 (81 GB -> 6.8 GB): `run21_40000` (previous serving model; the playground container still mounts it with `onnx/`), `run22_8000` (serving since 2026-10-19), `run20_best` and `checkpoint-3048` (weights only, no optimizer state) remain. The 18 light-run dirs in `checkpoints/headcmp/` keep `result.json` and `test_predictions.jsonl` (enough for paired tests) but no weights (`final/` deleted); the feature caches were deleted and are rebuilt on the next light run. `checkpoint-1524`, `run13_best_step1524` and `run21_36000` are gone.
- Never `pkill -f <script>` from a command whose own text contains that script name: it kills your own shell.
- genkai job logs (`*.sh.<jobid>.out`): tqdm lines arrive live but Python's stdout is block-buffered, so the `{'eval_ser': ...}` lines show up late. Read evaluations from the newest `checkpoints_*/checkpoint-*/trainer_state.json` instead. The system `python3` there is old (no backslashes inside f-string expressions), and `pjstat -H` lists finished jobs.
- Training speed on the 3090 (batch 16, bf16, measured on real clip lengths): random batches + gradient checkpointing 26 clips/s; `--group_by_length` 41; length-grouped with clips capped at 7 s and no checkpointing 56 (measured with foreach AdamW; the trainer's default fused AdamW is ~15% faster per step). Fused AdamW costs ~20 ms of a ~240 ms step, so the optimizer is not the bottleneck; a step is compute-bound at ~30 of 71 TFLOP/s. Without checkpointing, activations grow with batch x length^2 (eager `relative_key` attention), so a 7 s cap is needed on 24 GB. `--max_duration` does not exist yet.
- genkai GPU queue (2026-10-18): the GPUs were idle but every b-batch node had all 120 cores and ~907 GB memory booked (`pjshowrsc --rg b-batch -v 1` shows it), so a 1-GPU job (30 cores, 232 GB bundled) was estimated to wait ~20 h; c-batch was no better, `b-inter` takes interactive jobs only, and `b-batch-mig` slices (4 cores, 33 GB host, 11 GB GPU) start at once but cannot hold a full fine-tune of this model (hence `--train_top_layers 8`).
- genkai storage: random access into the shuffled train split (`select(indices)`, `flatten_indices`) ran at ~5 clips/s on Lustre; read the stored rows in order instead (`decode_train.py`, `compare_heads.py --sequential_features`; its features equal the normal path exactly, checked). The local box has little disk left (94% used): a full-data feature cache is ~290 KB per clip (~82 GB for 280k clips), so heavy training goes to genkai, not the local 3090.
- `compare_heads.py --group_by_length` also reorders the rows of `test_predictions.jsonl` (gold and pred stay paired, the order differs from the test split): match rows by their label sequence, not by index. Never put a `pkill -f` pattern into a command that contains the pattern; `pkill -f '[c]ompare_heads...'` avoids it.
